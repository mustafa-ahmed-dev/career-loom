# Cover Letter Tips

## Should you write one?
- A tailored letter beats both a generic letter and no letter. In one US field experiment of 7,287 applications: 16.4% callbacks with a tailored letter, 12.5% with a generic letter, 10.7% with none. [S156, Vendor]
- In a US survey of 200 hiring decision-makers, most said they read a letter even when it isn't required, yet only about 35–38% of candidates submit one. So a good letter stands out. [S157, Vendor]
- Another US vendor survey says about 60% of companies require one. [S158, Vendor]
- **There is no data for Iraq, Kurdistan or the wider Middle East and North Africa.** Ask whether a letter is expected.
- Google doesn't require letters [S024]; Harvard treats one as a screened writing sample [S020]; EU and UN processes expect a motivation letter [S041].

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Posting says a letter is required, or the upload field is mandatory | Tailored letter |
| Optional, and this is a top-priority application | Short tailored letter (250–350 words) |
| Optional, and many similar applications | Skip it; tailor the CV instead |
| Direct application, referral, WhatsApp or email | Short intro message |
| EU institutions, UN, academia, NGOs | Motivation letter or statement |
| Local employers in Iraq/Kurdistan | Short intro message by default; formal letter only if asked (unverified) |
| Any case | Never a generic letter |

## Structure (US/UK default)
1. **Opening (2–3 lines).** The role you're applying for, your strongest hook, and any referral in the very first line.
2. **Proof (1–2 paragraphs).** Match 2–3 of the employer's top needs to concrete evidence from your fact bank.
3. **Why this company.** One specific, sourced reason, not flattery.
4. **Logistics (optional).** Availability, relocation, visa.
5. **Close.** A clear next step ("I'd welcome a 20-minute conversation").
6. **Sign-off.**

Length: 250–400 words (US/UK). Address a named person when you can find one. [S020]

## Self-check (score each 0–5)
| Criterion | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|
| Company specificity | Mentions something only this company has |
| Proof | Concrete, verifiable evidence |
| Need-to-proof match | Each claim answers a stated need in the posting |
| Voice | Sounds like a real person, not a template |
| Close | A clear, polite next step |

A specificity score below 3 means rewrite the letter.

## Short variants
- **Email body:** a subject line plus 120–200 words.
- **LinkedIn note:** 300 characters or fewer.
- **WhatsApp intro:** 3–4 lines, then ask whether you can send your CV. Keep the attached PDF small enough to send easily.

## Don't
- Repeat your résumé line by line.
- Open with "I am writing to express my keen interest".
- Make claims about the company you can't source.
- Include charts or salary history.
- Quote the "125,000 applications" figure that circulates online; it misquotes the original study (see `myths-vs-reality.md`, #24).

## Round 3 additions (October 2026)
- AI-written letters weaken the signal: on one freelance platform, tailoring rose after an AI bid writer launched, but its link to callbacks fell by about half. Platform data, not salaried hiring. [S172, S173]
- Writing help is not the problem: a randomised study found non-generative writing help raised hires by about 8%. [S174]
- Keep requiring a concrete, verifiable detail from the user's own record in every letter.
