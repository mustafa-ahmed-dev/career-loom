# Myths vs Reality

How to use this file: when a user repeats a myth, give the reality in one or two sentences, with the source ID and evidence label. Correct the claim without making the user feel foolish.

| # | Myth | Reality | Sources | Evidence | What the plugin does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "75% of résumés are rejected by ATS software." | The figure traces to a 2012 sales pitch from a company that shut down a year later. Most ATS products parse résumés and rank or search them; true auto-rejection mostly comes from knockout questions that recruiters set up. | S001, S002, S003 | Moderate / Vendor | Reports a Parse-Risk & Findability Index, never "you'll be rejected" |
| 2 | "There's an ATS score, and you need to beat it." | ATS products don't publish a universal score. Some rank candidates against a job; recruiters mostly run keyword and filter searches. | S003, S011 | Vendor | Never shows a fake "ATS score" |
| 3 | "Recruiters spend exactly 6–7 seconds on a résumé." | The 7.4-second figure comes from a study that didn't disclose its sample or method. The direction holds: the first scan is short, and titles plus the top third of page 1 matter most. | S016, S005 | Weak | Puts the strongest content in the top third; never quotes the number as fact |
| 4 | "Most job postings are fake." | Overstated. Greenhouse classifies 18–22% of postings per quarter as ghost jobs, and about 1 in 3 employers in one survey admit posting without intent to hire. That is a substantial minority, not most. There is no data for the Middle East and North Africa. | S045, S056, S047 | Moderate / Vendor | Shows ghost-job warning signs, not verdicts |
| 5 | "If I'm qualified, they'll reply." | Silence is normal. In one large data set, only 3% of applicants were invited to interview, and most job seekers have been ghosted even after an interview. | S052, S043, S044 | Moderate | Reality-Check note; measures success as conversations per week |
| 6 | "7% of applicants are referrals but they make up 40% of hires." | No traceable primary source exists for this figure. The strong evidence (NY Fed study of one firm): referrals were 6% of applicants and 29% of hires. | S057 | Strong | Never uses the 7%/40% figure |
| 7 | "Networking is the only thing that works." | Referrals convert far better everywhere, but cold applications still produce many hires at large employers (52% of hires at Ashby customers came from inbound applications). | S057, S055, S045 | Strong / Moderate | Weekly plan balances applications **and** outreach |
| 8 | "In the Arab world, it's all wasta, so merit doesn't matter." | Personal connections are often the main channel (over 70% in Lebanon and Syria), but oil and gas companies, multinationals, UN/NGOs and government-linked employers still run structured screening. A strong document is still necessary. | S063, S064, S065 | Strong | Helps with legitimate referrals; refuses requests to bypass merit or offer payment |
| 9 | "XYZ is the formula Google and Meta require." | XYZ came from one LinkedIn essay by Google's former HR head (2014). It's useful, but no study shows it beats other formats, and Google doesn't require it. | S017, S018 | Weak (expert opinion) | Offers XYZ, CAR, PAR, STAR, scope-first, qualitative, or the user's own formula |
| 10 | "Every bullet needs a number." | Numbers help when they're real. Invented or inflated numbers fall apart in interviews and reference checks. Scope and recognition are valid evidence too. | S039, S020 | Strong (official guidance) | Truth gate: no number without a fact-bank entry |
| 11 | "Recruiters can always spot AI-written résumés." | Recruiters are confident they can, but in one test only 18% identified all the AI-written letters. What they actually penalise is generic writing. | S033, S026, S029 | Vendor | Voice Fidelity pass; never claims to be "undetectable" |
| 12 | "Use an AI tool that makes your résumé undetectable." | Detection isn't the real risk; generic, unspecific writing is. Specific, verifiable details are what work. | S029, S026 | Vendor | Requires one concrete detail per bullet |
| 13 | "PDFs can't be read by ATS." | Text-based PDFs parse well in most systems. Problems come from image PDFs, design-tool exports and complex layouts. Some legacy systems (Taleo is the one reported) handle .docx better. | S008, S009, S010 | Vendor / Weak | Exports both PDF and .docx; uses .docx for legacy portals |
| 14 | "Two-column résumés always get rejected." | Not always rejected, but they carry real parsing risk (reading order gets mixed). Fine for sending directly to people; risky for portals. | S011, S006 | Vendor | Two-column designs (tier C) always come with an ATS-safe twin |
| 15 | "Colour breaks ATS." | Colour itself doesn't break parsing; columns, tables, text boxes and graphics do. | S008, S007 | Vendor | Allows single-column colour designs (tier B) for portals |
| 16 | "A résumé must be one page." | It depends on seniority, field and region. Google sets no length limit; academic, UN, Japanese and many European formats run longer. | S024, S041 | Moderate / Strong | Region pack plus seniority set the length |
| 17 | "Cover letters are dead." / "Always send one." | Both are wrong. Google doesn't require one; Harvard treats it as a screened writing sample; EU and UN processes expect a motivation letter. | S024, S020, S041 | Moderate / Strong | Asks whether a letter is expected before writing one |
| 18 | "Hide keywords in white text to beat the ATS." | Recruiters see it once the text is extracted, and it signals dishonesty. | S003 | Vendor | Refuses hidden text |
| 19 | "Skill bars show how good I am." | "Excel 80%" has no defined meaning, and recruiters commonly mock it (a view shared by practitioners, not hard evidence). | S008 | Weak | Uses text levels only (Expert / Advanced / Working / Basic); no percentages |
| 20 | "A photo always helps." / "A photo always hurts." | It depends on region and role. Photos are discouraged in the US and UK, common in Germany/Austria/Switzerland and the Gulf, and expected for some appearance-related roles. | S089, S090, S080 | Weak (round 3 will verify) | Photo optional; suggested only for appearance-relevant roles or when the posting asks |
| 21 | "This tool compared your résumé against thousands of job descriptions." | No tool can honestly claim that for most markets, especially smaller ones like Iraq and Kurdistan. | S098, S076 | Strong / Weak | Always states "Sample: N postings from [sources], [dates], [location]" |
| 22 | "Write 'References available upon request.'" | It wastes a line; employers assume references are available. | S020 | Strong (official guidance) | Banned component |
| 23 | "Every résumé needs an objective statement." | Objectives focus on what the candidate wants. A summary of what you offer works better, except for students and career changers. | S020 | Strong (official guidance) | Objective allowed only for students and career changers |
| 24 | "ResumeGo studied 125,000 applications and found a 53.3% vs 34.8% interview rate." | Misquoted by AI cover-letter tool blogs. The original study had 7,287 applications: 16.4% callbacks with a tailored letter, 12.5% with a generic letter, 10.7% with none. | S156 | Vendor (field experiment) | Cites only ResumeGo's own page |
| 25 | "Nobody reads cover letters." | US vendor surveys say most hiring managers read them, even when optional, and tailored letters beat both generic letters and none. The real problem is generic letters. There is no data for the Middle East and North Africa. | S156, S157, S158 | Vendor | Tailored letter or none; short intro messages for direct channels |
| 26 | "Few companies use cover letters, so skip them everywhere." | Not supported for the US: vendor surveys report that most employers require or read them, and most candidates skip them. For Iraq/Kurdistan there is no data; one job seeker reports that letters are rarely asked for. | S157, S158, S159 | Vendor / Weak | Decision rules by situation; default to a short intro message for direct local applications |
| 27 | "Local employers in Iraq and Kurdistan all use ATS software." | No evidence found. One job seeker reports that few or no companies use ATS and that HR reads CVs manually. Unconfirmed. | S159 | Weak (anecdote, n=1) | Treat as a hypothesis; local employers may allow B/C designs, but keep the A twin for multinationals, NGOs and the UN |
| 28 | "A photo makes your application stronger." | Evidence is mixed and depends on country and gender: a premium for attractive men and a penalty for women in an Israeli field experiment, and penalties tied to name, headscarf or weight in German and Spanish ones. Photos are legally discouraged in some places. | S160–S164 | Strong (field experiments) | Photo stays optional and hidden by default; regional convention is information only |
| 29 | "Add every keyword from the posting and you will pass." | Keyword stuffing, white text and hidden prompts are detectable and risky, and 41% of US job seekers admitting prompt injection is a vendor survey, not proof that it works. | S215 | Vendor | Hidden-text detector; no hidden keywords, ever |
| 30 | "An Arabic-script CV will parse fine in any portal." | One major parser lists Arabic only for skills and ontology layers, not résumé parsing. Treat Arabic, Sorani and Persian-script CVs sent to portals as high parse risk. | S177–S180 | Vendor (primary documentation) | Send the English twin to portals; Arabic version to people |
| 31 | "AI writing help gets you hired less." | A randomised experiment found non-generative writing help raised hiring by about 8%. Separately, once AI writing became easy, tailored cover letters lost part of their signal value. Both findings come from one or two platforms. | S172–S174 | Moderate | Offer help, keep the user's voice, require concrete detail |
| 32 | "Everyone in Iraq gets hired through connections, so CVs do not matter." | Labour-market studies show informal recruitment is common in Iraq and Kurdistan, but there is no representative channel-share figure, and formal channels exist (KRG platforms). | S196–S203 | Strong (studies); channel share unmeasured | Reality note: CV is a tool inside a relationship-driven search |
