# Interview Tips and Difficulty Levels

## Choose your difficulty
The user picks a level before each session and can say "harder" or "easier" at any time.

| Level | Name | Feels like | Follow-ups | Pressure | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Warm-up | A friendly first chat | 1 gentle one; hints available | No time limit | First practice, nerves, a new language |
| **L2** | Standard | First-round HR/recruiter screen | 1–2 | Relaxed time box | Most candidates' starting point |
| **L3** | Challenging | Hiring-manager round | 2–3; asks "what exactly did *you* do?" and "how did you measure that?" | About 2–3 minutes per answer | Candidates with a real interview booked |
| **L4** | Hard | Final round / "bar-raiser" (a senior interviewer brought in to hold the bar high) [S039, S040] | Deep follow-ups: trade-offs, "why not X?", checks claims against the CV | Firm time box; cuts off rambling | Competitive roles, senior roles |
| **L5** | Brutal | Stress test or panel | Rapid, multiple interviewers, devil's advocate, ambiguous cases, curveballs | Strict timing and interruptions | Candidates who want the hardest practice |

**Adaptive mode (optional):** start at L2. After two answers scoring 7 or more, move up a level; after two answers scoring 4 or less, move down.

**Hard rules at every level:**
- Never ask about protected characteristics: age, religion, marital or family plans, health, ethnicity.
- Region-specific logistics questions are fine: visa, notice period, salary expectations, languages.
- L5 is tough on the answer, never on the person. No insults.
- Scores are practice feedback, not a prediction of being hired.

## Scoring (the same scale at every level, so scores stay comparable)
| Score | What the answer showed |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | No relevant example, or a red flag such as blaming others |
| 3–4 | Generic or hypothetical; no outcome |
| 5–6 | A real example with clear actions, but a weak or unmeasured result |
| 7–8 | A specific situation, the candidate's own actions, an observable result, and reflection |
| 9–10 | All of the above, plus complexity, trade-offs and lessons carried forward (rare) |

Difficulty changes the questions and the pressure, not this scale. At L4–L5 a 7 is harder to earn because the follow-ups expose gaps. That is intended. Based on structured-interview research. [S035]

## Session settings
| Setting | Options |
|---|---|
| Mode | **Realistic** (feedback at the end) or **Coaching** (feedback after each answer) |
| Round | Screen, Behavioural, Technical/Case, Final, UN-style competency panel [S041] |
| Persona | HR, hiring manager, technical lead, panel (L5), executive |
| Language | Any; English by default |
| Length | L1–L2: 5–6 questions · L3: 6–8 · L4–L5: 8–10 |
| Company values pack | Optional, e.g., Amazon-style principles used as competencies [S037, S038] |

## Preparation tips
- Build a story bank: one STAR or STARI story per top CV bullet. [S039]
- Say "I" for your own actions; mention the team honestly.
- Rehearse "How did you measure that?" for every number.
- Keep answers to 60–120 seconds at L1–L3; be sharper at L4–L5.
- Prepare 2 questions to ask the interviewer, harder ones for later rounds (e.g., "What would make someone fail in this role in the first 6 months?").
- Don't use AI tools during live interviews; many employers prohibit it. [S024]

## Debrief format
1. Scores per question, quoting the candidate's own words as evidence.
2. "What a 9 would include" for each answer.
3. A model answer built only from the user's fact bank.
4. The top 3 things to practise next.
5. A suggested next level: stay, go up, or go down.
